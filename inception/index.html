Ten hours of flight.

The film's rule sounds simple: each dream level runs about 20 times slower than the one above it. Apply it to one 10-hour flight and the arithmetic says 8.3 days, then 5.5 months, then 9.1 years. Yusuf rounds those to a week, six months and ten years. Below, the clocks drawn to scale, a converter, the kicks that have to land together, and the catch: the film actually states two different ratios.

The kicks

A kick, the feeling of falling, wakes a dreamer one level up. Yusuf: the trick is to synchronise a kick that can penetrate all three levels. Because the levels run at different speeds the kicks have to be timed to land together, so the climax cuts between all of them at once; Bordwell counts four embedded timelines framed by a fifth. source

  1. Level one Yusuf drives the van off the bridge; the kick is the moment it hits the water. Wakes everyone asleep in the van.
  2. Level two Arthur rigs the lift with explosives and fires it up the shaft while the hotel has no gravity. Wakes the team asleep in the lift.
  3. Level three Eames rigs the fortress with explosives and brings the floor down. Wakes the team asleep in the fortress.

Where the numbers come from

The film gives two ratios. Cobb: five minutes in the real world is an hour in the dream, which is 12 to 1. Yusuf, on the compound sedative: brain function in the dream will be about 20 times normal. Nobody says which one governs time at each level. source

Two rules, one worked example. At 12 to 1 (five minutes to an hour) the flight would be 5.0 days, 2.0 months and 24 months. At 20 to 1 it is 8.3 days, 5.5 months and 9.1 years. Yusuf says a week, six months and ten years, so the page is drawn at 20 to 1 and the converter uses it too.

The job is done on a Sydney to Los Angeles flight. Eames: we need at least a good ten hours. source

Yusuf works it out for the team: a week the first level down, six months the second, and on the third, ten years. Those round numbers fit the 20 to 1 rule, not the 12 to 1 one. source

Cobb and Mal spent something like fifty years in Limbo in one night, and Saito is an old man by the time Cobb finds him there. source

No source gives measured screen time per level in the climax. Bordwell counts the timelines and gives no minutes. Nor does anyone in the film say the 20 to 1 rule still holds in Limbo; fifty years in one night is a far larger ratio.
